# Grounded evaluation acceptance

PolicyNIM's preflight pipeline discovers policy IDs with ranked chunks, then supplies complete source context for those selected policies. A relevant intent paragraph can select a policy, but it can't substitute for that policy's required rules, conditions, or exceptions.

`top_k` limits discovery hits. The selection packet preserves their order, scores, and evidence. Generator-ready context appends every missing chunk for the selected IDs from the same pinned index. Added chunks have `score: null`; they weren't independently ranked. Each selection reason reports discovery and additional-source counts. This works with custom headings and doesn't add unselected, excluded, or superseded policy IDs.

The router rejects changed reranker metadata, unknown chunk IDs, inconsistent selected-policy metadata, and contexts above 80 chunks or 256 KiB of compact ASCII-escaped scored-chunk JSON. These are operational budgets, not provider limits. They include source metadata; compiler and generator requests also contain prompts and other fields, so this isn't a total request-size promise. Budget refusal uses `policy_context_budget_exceeded`, supplies no partial policy context, and asks the caller to narrow `top_k` or domain, or split reviewed policy documents. Identity refusal uses `policy_context_identity_mismatch` and asks the operator to preserve and inspect the generation.

The bounded context fixes a reproduced live failure: the cleanup-job required-rules chunk ranked second in dense retrieval, twelfth after reranking, and fell outside eight retained hits. The intent chunk remained, so guidance lacked the source's full conditions. The preserved ten-case result was 9/10. Passing structural tests doesn't change that result; a new live replay needs its own receipt and exact source/generation identity.

Existing acceptance gates cover profile replacements and exclusions through actual generation build, SQLite retrieval, routing, compilation, preflight, and `EvalService`; stale catalog/revision/chunk expectations reject before provider calls. Citation-coordinate checks bind results to sealed generation chunks. Historical reports capture their original suite and generation and can't be overwritten by changed suite files. Policy scenario tests require the authored applicable, violating, out-of-scope, and boundary fixtures with their reviewed revisions.

Offline provider doubles test orchestration, source inclusion, and structural refusal. Adversarial outputs can prove that the evaluator detects a selected bad result. They don't measure model reasoning, semantic correctness, or universal resistance to instructions inside retrieved text. Provider-message tests verify that retrieved text is serialized as untrusted data. Live NVIDIA search/preflight results are reported separately, with the selected model settings and generation. Conformance-model judgments are separate again; retrieval recall doesn't establish their accuracy. Skipped live tests aren't passes.

Generated guidance remains advisory. Review the cited source before applying implementation details, including numbers, APIs, and technology choices. Strict citation ownership and complete selected-policy context reduce specific structural failure modes; they don't independently verify every recommendation.
